Fire Detector Alarm

Thermistor-Based Fire Detection Circuit

A temperature-sensitive fire detection circuit built around an NTC thermistor, NPN transistor and 555 Timer IC. The system responds to rising temperature and activates a buzzer after the configured threshold is crossed.

Project Overview

This project demonstrates a temperature-sensitive fire alarm built from an NTC thermistor and supporting analog electronics. The thermistor detects a rise in temperature, the signal passes through a transistor switching stage, and a 555 Timer controls activation of a buzzer once the configured threshold is crossed.

Objective

  • Detect a rise in temperature that may indicate possible fire
  • Convert temperature change into an electrical signal
  • Trigger the 555 Timer when the configured threshold is crossed
  • Activate an audible buzzer
  • Demonstrate the operation of sensors, transistor switching and the 555 Timer

Limitations

  • Component tolerances can affect threshold accuracy
  • Electrical noise can influence sensing and triggering
  • Calibration is required
  • Sensor/circuit response is not instantaneous
  • Basic circuit has limited protection
  • Single heat-based sensing is less sophisticated than multi-sensor systems

Possible Improvements

  • More accurate sensor such as LM35 or DS18B20
  • Comparator such as LM393
  • Filtering or hysteresis
  • Test and mute buttons
  • Smoke/gas sensing
  • Better circuit protection
  • Improved physical enclosure
